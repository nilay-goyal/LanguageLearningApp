# Reviewing French subtitles: instructions

Thank you for helping. This takes about 30 to 40 minutes, and you can stop and come back.

## What this is

A language-learning tool shows English subtitles in which some words are swapped for French. A learner works out the French from the scene and the English around it, with no lessons. Your job is to say whether each swap is good French, in its place in the sentence.

Each row shows one item:

- **English line** is the original subtitle, with the lines before and after for context.
- **As shown** is what the learner sees. The French is between `<<` and `>>`, and the rest is still English. A full French line looks like `<<Je pars demain.>>`.

You are checking only the French between the arrows, and how it fits into its sentence. Don't worry about the English around it.

## What to put in the "verdict" column

Pick one for every row:

| Write | When |
|---|---|
| **OK** | Correct and natural French, and it means what the English means in this scene |
| **AWKWARD** | Correct but stiff, literal, or something nobody would really say. The learner would still understand it |
| **GRAMMAR** | Wrong French: agreement, verb form, a missing or extra word, or the swap breaks the sentence (for example "Did you remember nourrir the cat" loses the word "to") |
| **MEANING** | Good French, but it means something different in this scene, so a learner would learn the wrong thing. This is the most important one to catch. Look for words with two meanings |
| **REGION** | Fine in one kind of French but not another. Write which in the comment |

If a row has more than one problem, choose the more serious one: MEANING first, then GRAMMAR. The comment column is optional, and a few words on anything marked MEANING or GRAMMAR help a lot.

## Things to know

- **The target is French as spoken in France.** If you speak Canadian French and something sounds different, mark it REGION and say how it differs. Don't mark it wrong just because it isn't how you'd say it.
- **Short or casual is fine.** Subtitles are spoken dialogue, so "ça va ?" or "c'est pas vrai" are fine if they fit the scene.
- **Politeness:** if the speaker addresses someone as Monsieur or Sir, *vous* is right. Between friends, *tu*.
- **Don't look anything up** or ask anyone else. Your own instinct is what we want.
- **The text is from real shows.** Some lines contain swearing or adult themes.
- **Please don't share** the sheet. The subtitle lines belong to the shows' owners.

## Returning it

Save the sheet and send it back as a CSV, or share the Google Sheet. Thank you.
